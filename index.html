Weeks 3–6 temperature outlook experimental

How likely is each week to be warmer or colder than normal? "Normal" is the 1991–2020 climate of that place and time of year (CPC station analysis): a week counts as warmer than normal if its mean temperature is in the warmest 40 % of past weeks, colder if in the coldest 40 %, so with no forecast information both chances would be 40 %.

Method: the 16 runs of NOAA's CFSv2 seasonal model started on the date shown (4 runs at each of 00/06/12/18 UTC) are statistically corrected with a probabilistic bias correction in the spirit of Microsoft's PBC (Guan et al. 2026): for each 1° grid cell and week, the observed outcome is regressed on the ensemble-mean forecast over seven years of past CFSv2 forecasts (2019–2026) from the same time of year.

Skill is modest, as for all forecasts this far ahead: in leave-one-year-out cross-validation over 902 past forecasts the ranked probability skill score against climatology is (0 = no better than climatology, 1 = perfect). Part of the skill comes from the warming trend since 1991–2020. Rainfall at these lead times showed almost no skill, so it is not shown.

Reading the table. On this test rainfallAI ranks first of the thirteen 7-day forecasts on all five scores. NAM and HRRR forecast only 3.5 and 2 days, so their scores are averages of the easier first days and are not ranked. CESM, a 1° climate model, ranks last: coarse grids smooth out rain. WRF, ICON, MPAS and E3SM, which we also run ourselves, are being re-run for these dates and will be added. It ranks first on days 1–4 on IOA, SEEPS and every threat score; on days 5–7 ECMWF's AIFS is slightly ahead on SEEPS and IOA. It under-forecasts days of 1 inch or more at long range. The amount is the model's 75 % quantile, raised to 0.5 or 1 inch where its own probability of that much rain passes a threshold that depends on the lead day. The model and that rule were fixed on data before the test period, then scored here once.

ETS = equitable threat score; SEEPS = stable equitable error in probability space (WMO/ECMWF headline rain score, 0 = perfect); IOA = Willmott index of agreement (1 = perfect); MAE = mean absolute error of the daily total. This table is scored on a grid (MRMS radar + gauges), which is also what rainfallAI was trained on, so the independent station table above is the fairer everyday comparison. Re-scoring with conservative instead of bilinear regridding of the raw models changed no rank in an 8-model check. Several versions of the model were scored on this period while it was being developed, so its numbers here are slightly optimistic.

rainfallAI runs every day since 31 October 2026 (precipitation maps above, "AI · run here" group) and is ranked in the station-based precipitation table. The version in this table, with the ECMWF inputs, replaces the early-morning run each day from 1 November 2026, once those forecasts are out.

Rain probability test: 38 seven-day forecasts

How good is each forecast's chance of rain? Daily rain at lead days 1–7 from the same 38 starts as the rain test above (every third day, 1 July – 20 October 2026, 00 UTC), checked two ways: against NOAA MRMS radar + gauge analysis on the same 0.1° grid cells for every model, and against ASOS weather-station rain gauges (24 h ending 00 UTC; each forecast taken in the gauge's 0.1° cell). Ensembles give the share of their members that reach the amount (ECMWF ENS: 51 members, NOAA GEFS: 31); the National Blend of Models and rainfallAI forecast the probability directly. A single-run forecast gets a neighbourhood probability: the share of 0.1° cells in the 1.1° box around each point that reach the amount (box size fixed before scoring, the same scale as the rain test's FSS). Brier skill score: 0 = no better than always forecasting the test period's average chance of rain, 1 = perfect. ROC area: how well a high chance picks out the rainy days (0.5 = no skill, 1 = perfect). Ranked by Brier skill at 0.1 inch; best in bold.

Wind test: 38 seven-day forecasts at weather stations

10 m wind every 6 hours out to 7 days, from the same 38 starts as the rain and temperature tests (every third day, 1 July – 20 October 2026, 00 UTC), checked against the same ASOS/AWOS weather stations. AI models start from the data they were trained on, plus GraphCast and FuXi started from GFS. Ranked models are scored on exactly the same station times; ranked by speed RMSE, best in bold. Test period, 4 weeks ahead: weekly rain being filled in

The same 38 start dates (every third day, 1 July – 20 October 2026), now scored on weekly rain totals for weeks 1–4 (days 1–7, 8–14, 15–21, 22–28) against NOAA MRMS (0.1° grid, contiguous US). In each week every model is scored on the same starts and grid cells. Ranked by the skill score against climatology (MSESS: mean squared error relative to the 2021–2025 MRMS average for the same calendar week; above 0 = better than climatology). Models that stop at 15 days (GFS, ECMWF IFS, AIFS and the ECMWF ensemble) appear in weeks 1–2 only. rainfallAI-S2S is our 4-week model: a U-Net (3 trained copies averaged) that turns GEFS weekly rain, the MJO and ENSO into weekly totals, trained on the GEFS 20-year reforecast with NOAA AORC rain (2000–2019) and on 2021–2025 with MRMS, checked on separate months before this test; its statistical baseline weighs GEFS, CFS, the MJO and ENSO against climatology region by region; rainfallAI forecasts 7 days, so it appears in week 1 only (it is trained on MRMS, the truth used here, so the station table and the Stage IV check are its fairer tests); WRF cannot run 28 days (its GFS boundaries end at 16 days).

The models

Three kinds of forecast are shown. All of them start from the 00 UTC time of the day shown at the top.
AI · run here

AI weather foundation models, trained on decades of past weather (mostly the ECMWF ERA5 reanalysis). We run them ourselves every day on NVIDIA L40S GPUs, all started from the same NOAA GFS analysis, so differences come from the models alone. Each takes minutes rather than hours.

Physics · run here

Numerical models that solve the equations of the atmosphere on a grid. We run them ourselves every day on CPU nodes, also started from the GFS analysis. They range from a 12 km regional weather model (WRF) to global climate models (CESM, E3SM) run in forecast mode.

Downloaded

The official forecasts of national weather centres (NOAA, ECMWF, Environment Canada, DWD, Met Office, Météo-France, NASA, US Navy), downloaded each morning from their open-data services. Each starts from that centre's own data assimilation, which is usually the biggest advantage over the models we run.

Methods and caveats

  • Next 48 h (3-hourly) shows the first two days at 3-hour steps from the models that output that often (for example ECMWF IFS, NOAA GFS, Pangu-Weather's 3-hour model, WRF, CESM, E3SM and ICON, as available that day). Next 48 h (hourly) shows the models with hourly output. Map times are UTC with US Central time in brackets; precipitation is the 3 h (or 1 h) total ending at that time.
  • Temperatures are in °F, precipitation in inches, wind in mph. City charts are shown in each city's own local time (daylight saving included).
  • Verification: every model's forecasts from the last seven 00 UTC cycles (re-run as hindcasts where the daily run did not exist yet) are interpolated to ~2,400 ASOS/AWOS weather stations (Iowa Environmental Mesonet) and compared with the hourly reports at 00/06/12/18 UTC. Temperature, humidity and wind speed are ranked by root-mean-square error (RMSE, lower is better); the index of agreement (IOA, Willmott 1981: 1 − Σ(F−O)² / Σ(|F−Ō|+|O−Ō|)², 1 = perfect, 0 = no better than the observed mean) is shown for every variable except wind direction; wind direction by mean absolute angle error (MAE, only when the observed wind is ≥5 kt); bias is forecast minus observed. Precipitation compares 24 h totals at ASOS stations and ~19,000 CoCoRaHS volunteer gauges (24 h ending at the morning reading, matched to 12 UTC), and is ranked by the equitable threat score for ≥0.1 in (ETS, higher is better; 0 = no skill); ETS for ≥0.5 in (heavier rain) is also shown. All ranked models are scored on exactly the same station times. Stations measure one point while models predict a grid-box average, and no terrain-height correction is applied, so coarse models are penalized over mountains.
  • Short-range models (HRRR, NAM, RRFS, ARPEGE) stop at their last forecast hour. Models covering less than 97% of the stations are listed below the ranking with their own-domain score, so they do not change the other models' station sample.
  • "Day N" means hours 24(N−1)–24N after the start time. Maxima and minima use 6-hourly output, so true daily extremes may be slightly higher or lower.
  • Spread is the standard deviation across all models that predict a field. Large spread means the forecast is uncertain.
  • Most AI models do not predict surface humidity or rain. For them, surface humidity is estimated from the model's lowest pressure level above ground (1000/925/850/700 hPa), so humidity-based indices are approximate. Fields a model cannot provide are left out.
  • Indices: heat index (NWS Rothfusz); WBGT (simplified, shade); Fosberg Fire Weather Index; Hot-Dry-Windy Index (surface approximation: VPD × wind); reference ET from Hargreaves–Samani; 7-day P − ET₀ as the forecast's contribution to drying or wetting. Drought indices such as SPI or the US Drought Monitor need months of observations and are not forecast here.
  • The climate models (CESM, E3SM) cycle their land surface from day to day. CESM's land has come from a spin-up since the 31 Oct 2026 run; earlier runs had a cold-started land with a cold bias of about 8 °F, so its scores count only runs from 31 Oct on, and it is listed as not ranked until it has enough forecasts. E3SM's land starts from a 1-year spin-up from 30 Oct 2026, and its scores likewise count only runs from that date.
  • Data: NOAA/NCEP products are public domain. ECMWF IFS, AIFS and ensemble forecasts © ECMWF, licensed under CC BY 4.0 (ECMWF open data). Other centres' data are used under their open-data terms. Several AI model weights are licensed for non-commercial use only.
